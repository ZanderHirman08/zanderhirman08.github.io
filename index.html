Predicting Wildfire Risk in Colorado

Winter 2025
Google Earth Engine AlphaEarth Embeddings XGBoost

A wildfire ignition risk model built with a project partner (Kai Skowlund) for a graduate-level Machine Learning course, covering Colorado, New Mexico, Wyoming, and Utah. Combined 64-dimensional AlphaEarth satellite embeddings with environmental covariates (NDVI, climate, terrain, fuel, land cover) drawn from Sentinel-2, ERA5, USGS SRTM, LANDFIRE, NLCD, and VIIRS. Trained and compared five models (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, Neural Network). XGBoost with embeddings and environmental covariates performed best after threshold tuning, reaching 0.86 AUC and 0.83 recall, prioritizing recall to minimize missed fire risk. The resulting risk surface (pictured, about 14,000 grid points) could support wildland-urban interface preparedness and resource planning.

State-level statistical & spatial analysis

Income, Space, and Immigration

Fall 2024
R Spatial Statistics Random Forest

Final project for Statistics and Geographic Data at CU Boulder, examining how median household income relates to the distribution of foreign-born populations across U.S. states using 2020–2024 American Community Survey data. Found a Pearson correlation of r = 0.648 between income and percent foreign-born, and a multiple regression model, adding unemployment, labor force participation, population density, and border-state status, explaining 76.8% of the variation. Moran's I tests confirmed significant spatial clustering in both variables. Extended the analysis with a random forest model reaching 0.986 prediction R², a comparison against DHS lawful permanent resident flow data, and a county-level case study in Colorado.

Field water quality study

Water Quality & Urbanization in Boulder Creek

Spring 2025
Field Sampling R / Statistics Water Quality

A group field research project with Rianna Sjostrom, Sheldon Stone, and Brogan McGough, testing whether nitrate levels in Boulder Creek increase with surrounding urbanization. Sampled water at 6 sites along an urban-to-mountain gradient (4 samples per site, 24 total) using nitrate test strips. Urban sites averaged 21.66 ppm versus 2.5 ppm at mountain sites, well above the 10 ppm safety benchmark, a relationship confirmed with linear regression. Findings were compared against prior USGS and Wright Water Engineers studies of the same watershed.
